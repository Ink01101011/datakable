- Search: a one-word query with fuzzy matching on (the default) no longer splits every cell into words on each keystroke. The words of each cell are found once per data array, a block of rows at a time within the time slices, and kept (ex-search's word index, up to a cap: see the next point); a new data array finds them again on its first one-word query. At 100k rows a one-word keystroke goes from about 113–117 ms to about 66–71 ms, and at 1M rows from about 1.1–1.2 s to about 0.7–0.8 s (Node, median of fresh processes); with 4 searched columns of two- or three-word text, from about 111 ms (datakable 2.1.1 from npm) to about 75 ms. Whole-value matching (queries with spaces, `fuzzyMaxDistance: 0`) keeps one matcher over all rows, and a time slice now ends closer to its deadline: under a 4x CPU throttle the longest task of a first query over 100k rows goes from about 29 ms to about 18 ms, and of a first one-word query over 10 mixed columns from about 42 ms to about 23 ms. Results are unchanged: a randomised differential against a cold search covers edits, filters, query sequences and data spanning several blocks. (#96)
- Search memory: the kept words cost about 1 byte per word of a multi-word cell (none for a one-word cell) and are kept up to 1.5 MB per data array (100k rows × 4 columns of two- or three-word text need about 1.45 MB and keep it whole). Beyond the cap, on long text such as descriptions, the remaining rows have their words found again on every one-word keystroke, as in 2.1, with the same results. Every §9.3 scenario stays within 5 MB at 100k rows: 4.40 MB for filter + sort + a one-word query (was 3.93 MB, and 5.57 MB with ex-search 1.2.1's word index), 3.61 MB for a zero-config one-word query over 10 mixed columns (was 2.95 MB), 4.28 MB for one searched column of 30 words per cell and 4.51 MB for 100 words (6.21 MB and 12.61 MB without the cap). A one-word keystroke over 100k rows of 30-word cells takes about 148 ms (126 ms with every block kept, 200 ms with datakable 2.1.1), over 100-word cells about 475 ms (358 ms, 568 ms; Node, median of 5 fresh processes). Long text is also matched in fewer rows per time slice, so a first one-word query over 100k rows of 30-word cells no longer has an 88 ms task under a 4x CPU throttle (38 ms). For long text at large row counts, `fuzzyMaxRows` turns typo matching off and keeps no words. (#124)
- `@datakable/core` depends on `ex-search` 2.1.1 (was 1.2.1). ex-search 2.0 trims the query, no longer matches objects and arrays as text and skips `null` rows; DataKable already trims the query and hands ex-search its own formatted strings, so for valid options the rows and their order are unchanged (checked against results recorded with 1.2.1, including padded queries, `null` / `undefined` cells and object / array columns with and without a `search` weight). ex-search 2.x rejects a non-finite weight or threshold; a column `search: Infinity`, or in production a `NaN` / `±Infinity` `threshold`, searches as in datakable 2.1.1 (the same rows, order and status, checked against the published 2.1.1): DataKable hands ex-search finite values and scores those configurations as 1.2.1 did. New in development builds: a non-finite column `search` weight is reported as `DK_INVALID_OPTION` (`columns[i].search`), like a non-finite `threshold` already was. Matching now relies on ex-search's `search(query, { within })`: an `ex-search` older than 1.2.1 (forced through an override) gets the built-in exact / prefix / substring matching, from the ESM and the CommonJS build alike, reported in development with `DK_DEP_INCOMPATIBLE`. (#123)
